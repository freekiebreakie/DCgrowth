Where We Stand

A strong foundation in a fast-moving market.

We've been live for a couple of months and have been constantly in the top 10 across users, volume, and revenue among all Hyperliquid builders. That is a genuinely strong start. At the same time, we should not lose sight of how competitive this market is — new players rise every single day, and Hyperliquid's growing popularity continues to attract more and more builders.

As a response to that competitive pressure, we have differentiated ourselves by simultaneously becoming a HIP-3 deployer. This means we now compete on two distinct fronts: as the place where people trade — alongside players like MetaMask and Phantom — and as a deployer of the markets that people want to trade, alongside parties like Tradexyz and Hyena.

The position: We have a steady base of users, a strong application, a strong mobile application, and our markets are currently listed in the number two spot. The foundation is solid. Now we build on it.

Alongside this go-to-market plan, a wide range of product features and updates are in progress that further set us apart. Growth and product go hand in hand — more exposure amplifies what the product delivers, and a better product makes every growth effort more effective.

Pillar 1

Influencer marketing — finding partners that work.

In collaboration with Zoku, we are targeting a wide range of creators across the globe. The proof of concept is already there: collaborating with a French YouTuber, we reached a few hundred signups and over $10M in volume for $2,500 in fees paid. This market has huge potential — especially as we get more local and find the partners that truly convert.

The markets we are going after: South Korea and Japan are already proving to be strong areas within our current user base. Beyond that, we are targeting broader APAC, MENA, LATAM, and English-speaking markets.

The process is straightforward and repeatable:

  • Source a list of all potentially interesting creators
    Cast a wide net across all target markets and identify creators who speak to the right audiences — traders, investors, and financially engaged communities.
  • Negotiate the best deals
    Work with each creator to find an arrangement that makes sense — flat fee, commission-based, or a combination. The French YouTuber result shows that very efficient deals are achievable.
  • Prepare the videos and assist where necessary
    Support creators with briefings, product access, and anything they need to produce content that is both authentic and effective.
  • Launch the videos and analyze which creators perform best
    Track signups and volume generated per creator. The data tells us who is working and who is not — no guesswork.
  • Double down and bring the best on as affiliates
    Creators who perform well become long-term affiliate partners. This turns a one-off campaign into a sustained acquisition channel with aligned incentives on both sides.

Pillar 2

Increase our numbers — with features built for it.

Everything in this plan is ultimately geared towards growing our numbers. But some upcoming features are specifically positioned to do just that in a very direct way. Copytrading and Strategies are two features that optimize for increasing the number of trades — and with more trades comes more revenue.

That flywheel matters beyond just the revenue itself: when new users look at Dreamcash and see high trading activity and strong revenue numbers, it signals a healthy, active platform. That is compelling on its own and can be a meaningful driver of new user acquisition.

The flywheel: More trades → more revenue → a more attractive platform → more users → more trades.

Paid channels also fit naturally here. Shiny new features like Copytrading and Strategies give us something concrete and exciting to put in front of people. A well-timed paid push around the launch of these features — when interest and novelty are at their peak — is likely to perform better than broad, always-on campaigns.

Pillar 3

Fix the incentives — shift into second gear.

The incentives are working. When we announced the first season of rewards, things hockey-sticked quickly — users and volume spiked sharply. But things plateaued soon after. To get back into the minds of people and build momentum again, we need to revisit how the incentive program is structured.

This is a group discussion. The right direction here needs to be decided together. The options below are the ideas on the table — not a fixed plan.

The options we are considering:

  • Start a new season with completely different rules
    A fresh season with a different structure can reignite interest and give existing users a reason to re-engage. Changed rules also reset the playing field, which can bring in participants who felt the previous format did not suit them.
  • Use part of the current rewards for trading competitions
    Redirecting a portion of the rewards budget into competitions creates urgency, generates social content, and gives users a specific, time-bound reason to trade — all things the current incentive structure may lack.
  • Be more transparent
    Clearer communication about how rewards are calculated, distributed, and what users can expect builds trust and keeps the program front of mind.
  • Partner with more parties like Treadfi
    Expanding the ecosystem of partners involved in the rewards program broadens reach and can bring in new user segments we would not otherwise access on our own.

Pillar 4

Do all the necessary work — one by one.

Part of growth is straight-up manual work that simply has to get done. These are not glamorous initiatives — they are the foundations that make everything else more effective. We are currently working through these one by one.

SEO & GEO

Building organic search presence across our target markets so that people looking for what we offer can find us.

Email marketing

Setting up the infrastructure to communicate with our user base and bring people back into the product.

Enhancing our socials

Making our social presence stronger and more consistent across the channels where our audience spends time.

Paid traffic funnels

Setting up the foundations for paid user acquisition so that when we are ready to scale, the infrastructure is already in place.

None of these are quick wins on their own, but together they create a compounding base that makes every other growth effort more effective over time.

What this changes

Philosophy

Growing is adjusting.

One thing we must not forget is that we make a plan, but we can almost certainly say we will not see it through entirely. The items above are key ideas, not 100% concrete tasks — because a rigid checklist has proven not to be the best strategy.

We do not want to tick off giant lists of tasks. We want to steer in a steady line based on data, and tweak things accordingly. If we are on the right track, we double down. If we are not, we turn the other way.

The principle in practice: As new features roll out, it may well be that one of them suddenly sees a spike of usage — and that spike tells us where to steer our growth efforts next. Copytrading working? Run ads with a big budget and get people in. The same logic applies to anything, from Sherwood to a simple chat feature. Let the data lead.

This is not an excuse to be directionless. It is a commitment to being honest about what is working and disciplined about acting on it — rather than staying loyal to a plan for its own sake.
